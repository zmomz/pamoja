The idea
A city grows differently when people are seen as contributors, not recipients.

Pamoja means together. This is a collective rooted in newcomer knowledge, African Muslim traditions of hospitality, ethical relationship and mutual economic empowerment.

The website follows that philosophy: from the shared ground, into the roots, through the trunk, across the branches, and toward what is still growing.

01 · Soil

The Ground We Share

Three reference points shape the ground Pamoja stands on: the Dish With One Spoon Wampum, the Two Row Wampum, and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms.

These Indigenous teachings are learned from and honoured. They are not Pamoja's property, and they are not decorative motifs.

02 · Roots

Our Roots

Ta'aruf is ethical, dialogical relationship: hospitality, dignity, difference without erasure and the time it takes to know one another.

Takaful is mutual economic empowerment and collective stewardship.

These roots feed Pamoja's own practice while remaining distinct from the teachings represented by the shared ground.

03 · Trunk

Who We Are

Pamoja grew from Sudanese traditions of hospitality and the fertile ground of the Community Permaculture Lab — from a biweekly tea gathering into a collective.

Its work braids decolonial education, intergenerational dialogue, mutual economic empowerment and community connection.

05 · The canopy

What We Offer

Institutions can engage Pamoja's expertise and the knowledge carried by its community through workshops, hospitality rituals and consultation.

Build trust first. Coffee, tea, stories and understanding come before agendas.

Take only what you need. Leave something for others. Keep the dish clean.

06 · The grove

Who We Grow With

A tree doesn't grow alone. Pamoja names partners for what they actually contribute — space, funding, teaching, relationships and shared work.

Settlement organizations are collaborators, not competitors. Their work makes this practice possible while Pamoja contributes something distinct.

07 · Seeds

What's Growing

Free or low-cost space. Flexible multi-year trust-based funding. Legal, financial and administrative capacity. Relationships that let good work take root.

Sometimes investment looks like co-hosting a community meal, lending expertise, sharing unused space or helping with a crowdfunding campaign.
